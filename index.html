metabeta

In-context Bayesian inference for hierarchical data.

Grouped tabular data is everywhere; GLMMs allow statistical inference on it. Bayesian models incorporate prior knowledge and properly quantify uncertainty. But fitting them (repeatedly) with Hamiltonian Monte Carlo is expensive.

Our bespoke neural model combines Laplace approximation, Set Transformers, Neural Spline Flows, and Independence Metropolis-Hastings. It takes data, models and priors as in-context inputs, and samples from the full posterior.

10²–10³× faster than NUTS median wall-clock time for data with up to 3,000 observations

Across controlled benchmarks and real datasets, metabeta closely matches NUTS in parameter recovery, calibration, posterior geometry, and out-of-sample prediction. This still holds in stress tests including misspecification, distribution shift, collinearity, and data-poor regimes.

metabench

Sparse LLM evaluation: asking the right set of questions.

Evaluating a language model on large benchmarks like MMLU takes thousands of responses. Many of those carry redundant information. We found a method for picking the most informative subset of questions for 6 widely used benchmarks.

We fit item-level responses from thousands of LLMs using Item Response Theory, separating item properties and model abilities. We then used Fisher-information filtering to select the most informative items along the ability spectrum. The latent-ability estimators from the resulting subsets turn out to be excellent for reconstructing the original benchmark scores.

<3% of the original size 28,632 test items of the Open LLM Leaderboard

Our method recovers the held-out scores with a mean RMSE of 1.24% across 6 benchmarks (ARC, GSM8K, HellaSwag, MMLU, TruthfulQA, and WinoGrande). The joint score is reconstructed with an RMSE of 0.58%.

Open-source research software

scamd

A standalone Python simulator that generates realistic synthetic tabular datasets for regression. It draws covariates from structural causal models (implemented as a random MLP or a sparse DAG). The pipeline then applies post-hoc transforms to produce mixed continuous, categorical and count features. Used in metabeta's simulator.
